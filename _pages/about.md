I am an undergraduate researcher in the Nonlinear Dynamics Lab at UNIST, advised by Prof. Pilwon Kim. I build data-driven digital twins of nonlinear dynamical systems using reservoir computing.

My current work asks a practical question about critical transitions: _given the statistics we can actually measure from a system, how far can it drift before its normal dynamics break down?_ I am developing **statistically conditioned digital twins** that use measurable quantities, such as fluctuation amplitude, autocorrelation, and permutation entropy, as coordinates of the dynamical regime. These twins are trained only on records from normal regimes. Continued beyond the training range, they locate where normal dynamics stop persisting without ever seeing a transition.

So far I have applied this framework to ecological collapse in a chaotic food chain, voltage collapse in a power system, explosive synchronization in oscillator networks, and seizure onset in the Epileptor model.

**Interests:** nonlinear dynamics · reservoir computing · tipping points and early-warning signals · data-driven modeling
